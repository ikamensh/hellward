# Hellward — one defence

Hellward is a gothic tower defence about protecting a sanctuary lamp. Demons approach through branching halls, and their leaders curse the towers defending it. A leader chooses its curse by simulating the fight ahead. [The campaign](campaign.md) connects twelve defences across two acts; [the story](story.md) gives their cast and stakes.

Ilya's original brief (2026-09-24) asked for Diablo-inspired monsters and magic, doors that hold enemies while they break, and leaders whose curses are almost optimal rather than arbitrary. The later progression redesign starts much smaller: a few arrows against enemies with single-digit life, then hard choices about when to buy power and when to save resources for future fights.

## A defence

- **Map and movement.** Each 33 × 18 map separates monster halls from tower plots. The halls branch around buildable islands and rejoin at the sanctuary; monsters stay on their walkable floor, and towers stand only outside it. Tristram has several routes from one entrance. Every later location has two ordinary entrances, a direct route and a longer loop from each, and some have a sealed third entrance. A monster commits to one route when it spawns. Fallen, skeletons, zombies, goatmen, spiders, hulks and drowned may take a seeded loop; fast enemies and leaders run their authored direct route. A tower attacks by actual distance, and enemies on different routes are ordered by how far they remain from the sanctuary.
- **Opening power.** Tristram offers only the single-target Arrow Tower and Smite. An Arrow costs 12 gold at the opening and hits for 2; its later ranks hit for 3 and 4. The first Fallen has 6 life. The first Shaman has 12 before wave growth. All opening hits and enemy life use the 1–20 scale.
- **Growth.** `sim/balance.py` holds the common knobs: base life 6, 5.5% growth per location, 5% growth per wave, Arrow hit 2, and a starting budget of three rank-I Arrows plus half an Arrow unit per later location. Legacy swarm sizes shrink with campaign depth and stop shrinking at 40%, keeping single-target play viable. Enemy roles and tower roles multiply the common scale. A wave's gold budget starts at one local Arrow-price unit and grows by half a unit per wave; kills and the clear reward share it, so a larger swarm does not create unlimited money. Prices rise with the local gold unit. Bosses and optional elites add encounter multipliers.
- **Towers.** Arrow, Pyre, Storm Obelisk, Frost Shrine and Plague Totem attack or afflict one target at first. Pyre's blast, Storm's jump, Frost's burst, and the major spells arrive later through skills, forging, or location unlocks. Bone Altar marks a knot of enemies to take more damage; Druid Grove lends a nearby tower a damage bonus. Neither support tower deals damage directly. Every tower has three ranks; skills unlock the upper ranks, and battle gold pays for them. The [campaign](campaign.md) lists the unlock order.
- **The physical family.** Physical towers carry no element (armor is what they fear) and share the Steel column's rank skills. The Arrow is the cheap light hitter. The Ballista (from the Catacombs) looses a heavy bolt slowly, 7/10/14, at the monster nearest the sanctuary: the general answer to armor. The Hook Tower (from the Docks) hooks, every 6/5/4 seconds, the foremost small monster (one life, no boss, no leader) at least a tile past its spot (each route's point nearest it), drags it 2 tiles back toward the spot, never past it, and hits it; it takes flyers, never a monster at a gate, never one it has hooked before, and a walker it drags into a standing gate's arch stops at the queue. Weaken shortens the pull to Weaken's damage share. The Knife Post (from the Docks) throws at a small monster standing at a gate or held, if one is in reach, twice as hard, and otherwise at the foremost: a gate becomes a kill zone for chaff. A monster records each kind of mover that has moved it, so each moves it back once.
- **Damage.** Every hit (a tower's attack, a bolt's impact, a leap, a splash's lesser blow, Meteor, Frozen Orb) goes through one function, `content.felt_hit`: its factors multiply (the element: ×0.75 on a monster *protected* against it, ×1.25 on one *vulnerable*; the Altar's amplification; the Grove's aura; the knife's ×2; a leap's decay; a splash's 0.6), capped at ×2; the hit is rounded to a whole number, up when the product is above 1, down when below, half up at 1; the monster's armor comes off; at least 1 is dealt. Venom and the burning floor take the element and the amplification, with no armor and no rounding; Smite, Thorns and the death bursts take neither. A kind has at most two element tags (a boss more); nothing is immune. The Overlord, the Zealot and Azazel wear 2 armor, the Hulk and the Bone Priest 3, and each armored ordinary kind keeps a vulnerability, so an element has a way in. The planner's estimate and the server's hover table use the same function.
- **Gates.** Gargoyles and bats fly over gates. A warded gate costs one local Arrow-price unit and has base 70 life before location and skill scaling. It stops only walkers whose trail crosses its arch; others can go around. A standing gate mends after a wave. A broken gate cannot be rebuilt until the wave break.
- **The shrine.** A monster that reaches the end of its route strikes the shrine and costs its lives. An ordinary monster is obliterated. A boss (Azazel, the Bone Priest) costs 5 lives a strike and is sent back to its portal with its life and afflictions, to walk again; it counts its strikes.
- **Leaders and counterplay.** A curse falls on a marked spot and catches every tower in its radius. Weaken cuts damage, Decrepify cuts attack rate, Dim Vision cuts reach, and Bone Prison silences. Nothing lifts a curse and no spell breaks a chant: the answers are spacing, killing the leader before its curse lands (Smite can finish one), and choosing what to boost. Battle Hymn (from the Graveyard, 40 mana, 15 s to gather again) makes one tower attack twice as fast for 6 seconds, and the leaders see the stronger tower: a hymned tower draws their curses. The Inquisitor marks its spot for longer instead of chanting.
- **Optional risk.** At six locations, a sealed breach can add a named elite and harder pack to the next wave. The player declines, chooses a battle-gold cache, or seeks a forging trophy. Kills pay gold immediately. A few ordinary enemies also drop salvage: selling it during a break buys power now; banking it on victory moves a forged tower pattern closer. See [campaign](campaign.md) for the persistent rewards and recipes.

The opening has no damaging area attack. The earliest optional Pyre blast requires saved trophies and salvage at the Docks; the first skill route to area damage and Frozen Orb opens in the Jungle. Strong damage remains concentrated in invested ranks and scarce patterns.

## How a leader chooses (`sim/planner.py`)

When its curse is ready, a leader asks its planner. The world is copied at a step boundary and the decision is read after a visible pondering delay. The game computes the answer in a worker process; the same seed and state give the same answer inline or in a replay.

1. **Candidates:** each curse the leader knows at every reachable tower's spot. The spot's radius includes nearby towers.
2. **Estimate:** project each monster on its committed trail, including gate stops, and estimate the damage each candidate prevents, every hit felt through `felt_hit` (protections, armor, auras), a hymned tower at its hymned rate, a Knife Post's doubled knives on the small monsters queued at a gate, and a Hook Tower's pulls by the damage the other towers deal the dragged monster in the time the pull gives them. The ten strongest candidates proceed.
3. **Rollouts:** clone the real world and step it at 0.1 s for the curse and a short aftermath, once without a curse and once per candidate. Compare monster life still on the field, life leaked to the sanctuary, damage to gates, and how long the pack stays alive.
4. **Timing:** also try the best candidates two and four seconds later. Hold a curse only when waiting has enough measured value.

The rollout shares the game's rules for route movement, tower reach, gates, the damage pipeline, the hook's pulls, the hymn, forged patterns, salvage and optional packs. The estimate is cheaper; `tools/curse_quality.py` checks how well its shortlist and the final choice perform. Compare quality and balance measurements only when they use the same profile and campaign rules; earlier tables predate this redesign.

## Showing the leaders' minds

A pondering leader shows dots. A chant draws a growing violet sign at its target; a mark burns on its spot before it lands. The chronicle names the leader's choice and estimated gain, and the optional leaders' minds overlay writes candidate gains by the towers. A cursed tower wears a spinning sigil, a hymned one a golden aura. Hovering a monster shows its name, life, armor, the elements it is protected against and vulnerable to, the hit each of the player's tower kinds deals it, and, for a leader, its curses.

## Look and sound

The battle is drawn in 3D by the Godot client (`godot/`, [the client and the server](godot-client.md)): modelled and animated monsters and towers (tinted stand-ins for the kinds still without a model), each location's own scenery, the leaders' pondering motes, chant beams and rune circles, and a HUD of orbs, slots and tower cards. See [audio](audio.md) for the sound system.
