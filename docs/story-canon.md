# Hellward — story canon

What is *true* in the fiction, separate from how it is told. `hellward/story.py`,
the location words (`hellward/sim/locations/*`: taunt, lesson, blurb, wave names)
and the intro script (`tools/intro.py`) are the telling; this file is the thing
they must stay consistent with. Every claim has a tag; critiques cite tags (as
`canon LAMP-3`), so a canon change can find every beat it touches. Anything with
**[OPEN]** is a hole: file it here first, then fix the telling — never the reverse.

## The world

**Doors.** Hell is below, and in some places the rock between is thin. Those
places are doors. [DOOR-1]

**Lamps.** The saints built on every door and hung a lamp over it. While the
lamp burns, the door stays shut; the light itself is the bar — what is down
there fears it. [LAMP-1] Every lamp was lit from one flame, the mother lamp in
the Temple of Light at Kurast. If the mother lamp goes out, every lamp lit from
it goes out. [LAMP-2]

**The lantern.** Akara lights the keeper's lantern from the lamp of Tristram at
the well, on the first night. From then on the keeper carries the lamp's own
flame into every fight. [LANT-1] Lamps burn oil; the lantern burns on the
keeper's keeping. A flame given into keeping burns on keeping from that hour —
oil cannot starve it afterwards. That is why it survives the mother lamp's
going out, when every oil flame lit from her dies. [LANT-2]

**The chain.** When the mother lamp goes out, every daughter dies with her;
when she burns again, every daughter catches with her. Akara's candle burns
through the dark on keeping alone, and beside it she watches Tristram's lamp
catch. [MOTH-1]

**The keeping.** The keeping passes from keeper to keeper, night by night;
tonight it is yours. Akara can mind a lamp, but the old towers answer only the
keeper. [KEEPER-2] The keeping holds its keeper past death: it held him long
while he lived — nine hundred years alive — and when he stopped keeping, he
died of it, but the keeping would not let him go. That is why he walks still,
a skeleton who cannot lay the office down. [KEEP-1]

**What each defence defends.** Every map ends at a sanctuary chapel, and the
keeper sets the lantern down burning in it while working. The red orb is that
flame: every demon that reaches the sanctuary dims it; if it goes out, the
night is lost. This is true on all twelve maps in both acts — the orb is never
anything else. [ORB-1]

**Towers.** The saints' old works sleep in hallowed ground, and they answer
whoever keeps the lamp — so they answer the keeper. Where nothing sleeps, the
keeper raises new works, paid for in the faithful's gold. Waking and raising
are the same office: the old house answers, and the keeper builds where it
does not. [TOWER-1] Kurast's ground is hallowed too — the mother lamp's own
city, temple ground a thousand years old — so towers rise there as well, and
they know the keeper by Tristram's flame in the lantern. [TOWER-2]

**Bones.** The bone-casting is the art of Rathma's order: the bones show all
that is settled — every stone, every tower, every demon of a fight. They never
show the keeper, because the keeper bears the flame, and the bones cannot show
the light itself. The priest stole the art; the necromancer lends it back as
the Bone Altar. [BONES-1]

**The dead.** The buried can be called back, but each calling spends them: every
time, fewer answer. Raising is not free, even for him. [DEAD-1] The keeper's
own dead are the Plague Totem's work — venom raised from the slain; that is the
prologue's "and its dead." In Kurast the necromancer lends Rathma's art back as
the Bone Altar, which the keeper raises as a tower. [DEAD-2]

**Breaches.** Some maps hold a sealed side door — a crypt, an ossuary, a choir
unopened for years. The keeper may break the seal for the gold or the trophy
inside, at the cost of whatever walks out with it. The first seal is in the
graveyard. [BREACH-1]

**Gold, mana, gates.** Gold is the faithful's offerings, gathered as the night
goes on; the keeper spends it to raise new works. Mana is the keeper's prayer,
gathered with it and spent on spells: Smite (holy lightning), Meteor, Frozen
Orb, Battle Hymn. [COIN-1] At the old arches the keeper can raise a warded
gate — a blessed barrier the host must break through; warding is building.
"Doors" in the telling always means gates or literal doors, never the
tear-portals the monsters walk out of. [GATE-1]

## The people

**The keeper (you).** Tonight's keeper of the lamp of Tristram. Never named,
never clearly seen — a hooded shadow with a lantern. Flame-bearers dream true:
the keeper dreams the lamp's places before walking them. [KEEPER-1]

**The Bone Priest.** Nine hundred years the cathedral's priest and keeper of its
lamp; dead and not gone. Every night he cast his bones before the door, and
every night they showed the lamp going out. Somewhere in those years he stopped
keeping the lamp and began to love the future the bones showed — he serves it
now, the way he once served the lamp. That is his whole motive: certainty
become worship. What he wants is an end: he cannot lay the keeping down
[KEEP-1] — so he will destroy what it keeps. It explains the bargain with
Azazel, the host sent upward, the flight east, and the draining of the mother
lamp a little each night. [PRIEST-1]
His tells: "your saints" (never "ours"), and "my lamp" (it was his charge; the
saints were never his people). [PRIEST-2] His arc: certain, annoyed, curious,
afraid, bargaining — and at the end, seeing the keeper at last, content to be
dust. [PRIEST-3] Seeing the keeper — the keeping truly held — releases him;
nine hundred years catch up at once, and he is dust. [REL-1] The crown in
every panel is his own doing: when he stopped keeping he crowned himself —
keeper no longer, but king of what comes through. [CROWN-1]

**His voice.** His taunts run ahead of every host, carried by his acolytes on
the eve of battle; the briefings stage them as his voice. That is how he
taunts fights he does not attend. His taunts menace and sometimes bluff;
pages and lessons teach the truth. [TAUNT-1] The prologue's beam is his will
shown plainly, in the visions' figurative register; in battle the curses fall
through his acolytes and leaders — he fights in person first, and only, at
the temple. [CURSE-1]

**The flame answers.** The towers answer the flame-bearer. The Council may
give him the keeping of the lamp, but his hands are empty, and the old towers
know it. [FLAME-1]

**The leash.** Azazel was the Flayers' lord; dead lords hold no leash. The
priest holds it now. [LEASH-1]

**The elders.** On the terrace the curse-casters are the elders: "elders" in
the Travincal words means the leaders the player fights, whatever their kinds.
Accepted veil — the white-and-gold models carry it. [ELDER-1]

**The Council.** The High Council kept the mother lamp carelessly for years; its
oil was already low when his letter came. The Zakarum church has fallen — its
zealots serve the coming dark knowingly — which is why the Council let him in
and fights for him openly. The secrecy of the draining is for the city, not
the Council: Kurast must not notice until it is dark. [COUNCIL-1]

**His plan, in order.** First the nearer door: open Tristram's, let Azazel
through, and let the lamp go out in the flood. Only when the door shut on
Azazel did he sail east for the mother of lamps. [PLAN-1]

**Akara.** A priestess of the Sisterhood. She finds the keeper at the well,
lights the lantern, and reads the signs. At the caves she stays behind to keep
the lamp while the keeper goes below; at the end of Act I she keeps Tristram
while the keeper sails east. Her blessing is the free unlearning in the tree.
[AKARA-1]

**The necromancer.** A Priest of Rathma, met at the Kurast Docks. The Bone
Priest stole his order's art; he lends it back as the Bone Altar. [NECRO-1]

**The eldest druid.** Keeper of the oak ring in the Spider Forest. The oaks
remember the saints who lit the lamps. He brings the Grove, and at the end the
oil that relights the mother lamp. [DRUID-1]

**Azazel.** A lord of hell waiting at the door, promised his hour by the priest.
He falls in the doorway at the end of Act I. [AZA-1]

## What happens

### Act I — the descent

The door under Tristram is opening. The priest sends his host *up* toward the
lamp while he goes *down* to open the door himself. The keeper bars the way
with the lantern burning, then follows him down. Each victory pushes the fight
one step deeper. [DESCENT-1]

- **Tristram.** The lamp guttered at midnight; the village burns now. Akara
  lights the lantern at the well and gives the one order: keep it tonight. The
  Fallen come up through the cellars, driven by a shaman — with a few red
  jungle knives among them, bought from the south. [TEASE-1] After: the carved
  finger bone — this was sent. (Promise opened: who sent them.)
- **Graveyard.** The priest steps into the open, calling the buried by name.
  After: the tally wall — he has watched this fight every night, and in every
  night the lamp goes out. (Promise opened: the dare.)
- **Cathedral.** The lamp's own nave, held by the enemy: goatmen in the pews, a
  witch singing vespers. The keeper dreamed this room. After: the stair down,
  the warm stub — he went ahead, unhurried. (Promise opened: the stair.)
- **Catacombs.** Nine hundred years of the faithful, unstacking; Overlords from
  below. After: his cell — the bones laid out as a path through every fought
  room, its last stretch bare, leading to the door. (The dare, restated.)
- **Caves.** Gargoyles over lava; Akara stays to keep the lamp. After: the worn
  steps — every night for nine hundred years he cast his bones here, and
  somewhere he stopped keeping and began to wait. (Motive, shown not told.)
- **Hell's Gate.** The door stands open a hand's width; Azazel waits in the
  fire. The priest speaks his defiance. After (act ending): Azazel falls, the
  door shuts; the bones never showed the keeper; the priest goes east; the
  mother lamp is named; the keeper takes Tristram's flame east. (Promises
  closed: the door, the dare's first half. Opened: the mother lamp.)

### Act II — the drowned temples

The priest fled east by ship to drain the mother lamp quietly, a little each
night, so no one notices until it is dark. The keeper follows with Tristram's
flame in the lantern — which is why the old towers know the keeper abroad.
[DRAIN-1]

- **Docks.** Kurast; the necromancer lends back the stolen art. After: he is
  not raising an army, he is spending one. (DEAD-1, shown.)
- **Spider Forest.** Webs, bats; the druid and the oak ring. After: he passed
  three nights ago; the trees did not like him. (The trail.)
- **Jungle.** Zealots of the fallen church, inquisitors who curse without
  chant. After: the letter — "Your lamp gutters, as mine did. Let me keep it
  for you." The Council let him in. (Promise opened: the Council is his.)
- **Drowned City.** The sunken half of Kurast; Thorned Hulks out of the
  drowned. After: lamp oil in the shrine water — he is draining the mother
  lamp. (DRAIN-1, shown.)
- **Travincal.** The High Council waits on the terrace, every elder cursing.
  After: the doors shut by themselves; bones cast behind them, again and
  again. (Dread before the last door.)
- **Temple.** He fights himself, to look at the keeper at last. After (act
  ending): the vision holds the keeper; "There you are," and dust; the mother
  lamp goes out; Tristram's flame and the grove's oil relight it; across the
  sea, Akara watches the small red flame stand straight. (All promises closed.
  Last line: "Keep it burning.")

## Why this story

- The villain teaches the game: every intro detail (lamp, carpet, towers, bones,
  violet, the bell) points at something the player will meet. The fiction *is*
  the tutorial.
- The planner is made fiction: the one new mechanic (an enemy that simulates the
  fight) becomes the one new story (an enemy who has watched every fight).
  His arc is the difficulty curve with a voice.
- The dare structure: "In every night I have seen, the lamp goes out" states the
  goal as a dare, and "Keep it burning" answers it. The campaign is one long
  refusal.
- Few words between fights: at most three short paragraphs a page, one notch of
  plot per after-page. The story must never keep a player from the next defence.
- One orb, everywhere: the lantern's flame. The player never has to ask what
  they are defending again after the first night.

## Expected impression, beat by beat

- **Prologue:** awe, then unease (the narrator is not on your side), then the
  dare. The player leaves knowing: the lamp, the towers, the bones, violet.
- **Tristram:** urgency and trust — the village burns *now*, Akara gives one
  order, the fight teaches spreading. After: the first chill (a priest's gift
  among the dead — this was sent).
- **Graveyard:** the priest steps into the open (before), names his method
  (the tally wall: he has watched this fight). The player learns gates.
  Impression: he is losing, and keeping score.
- **Cathedral:** arrival at the lamp's own nave; the enemy holds the holiest
  ground (blasphemy as set dressing). After: the hunt turns downward (the
  stair, the warm stub).
- **Catacombs:** the weight of the dead (nine hundred years, unstacking) and
  the first thing from *below* (Overlords). After: intimacy — his cell, his
  path of bones, the bare last stretch. The dare restated as a map.
- **Caves:** the threshold of hell (lava, wings); farewell to Akara. After:
  pity and dread — the worn steps, nine hundred years, the turn from keeping
  to waiting.
- **Hell's Gate:** the door itself, Azazel in the fire, the priest's open
  defiance. Ending: triumph (the door shuts), mystery (he never saw you),
  widening (the mother lamp), departure (take the flame east).
- **Docks:** a new shore, a new ally; the stolen art lent back. After: the
  turn — he is spending, not raising. First hope he can run out.
- **Spider Forest:** old green powers taking sides. After: the trail is warm
  (three nights ago).
- **Jungle:** the enemy's church in white and gold; curses with no warning.
  After: the letter — he talked his way in. He is not only strong; he is
  plausible.
- **Drowned City:** drowned grandeur, unstoppable Hulks. After: the quiet
  horror — he is draining the mother lamp a little each night.
- **Travincal:** the whole Council against you; his bargain offered to you
  (leave Tristram's lamp — no one would miss it). After: shut doors, bones
  behind them. No more talk.
- **Temple:** he fights himself, to see you. Ending: recognition ("There you
  are"), release (dust), darkness, relighting, and across the sea the small
  flame standing straight. Last line lands the dare's answer.

## Review principles

1. **Canon-consistency.** No beat may contradict a canon claim; no beat may rely
   on an [OPEN] hole without naming it. Mechanics-fiction fit counts: what the
   player *does* (build, leak, breach) must be sayable inside the canon.
2. **Establishment-before-use.** Every proper noun, creature and concept a beat
   uses must be established in an earlier beat — or in the same beat, on purpose.
3. **One notch per after-page.** Each after-page (or ending) opens or closes
   exactly one story question. Before-pages set dread; briefings characterize.
4. **Words-panel correspondence.** The panel shows what the text says: same
   subject, same state, same moment. Neither side may add a fact the other
   contradicts.
5. **Voice discipline.** Before-pages present tense, after-pages past; only the
   Bone Priest says "your saints"; only he claims "my lamp"; the keeper is never
   named or closely seen.
6. **Vibe coherence.** Words, panel, level still and music blurb serve one mood
   per location. Name the intended mood before judging.
7. **Promise ledger.** Every question a beat opens (dawn questions, the seal, the
   stair, the mother lamp) is tracked until a beat closes it or the canon
   declares it open. No silent drops.
8. **Blind comprehension.** After a telling pass, an agent that has walked only
   the beats and panels — never this canon, never the sources — must be able to
   report the canon's core (the lamp, the lantern, the priest's motive, the
   bones' blindness) unprompted. If it cannot, the telling failed, not the agent.

## The loop (one iteration)

1. **Walk** the agreed beats (`tools/llm_story.py`; open every panel).
2. **Critique** against the eight principles, citing beat keys and canon tags.
   New holes go to Open questions first — the telling is not fixed around them.
3. **Propose** minimal edits, ordered: canon decision (if a hole) → words →
   repaint (`tools/story.py paint --only KEY`, one panel, costs credit) →
   stills regen (`llm_story.py stills`) → tooling (`llm_story.py` + its tests).
4. **Approve**: the user approves canon and words changes; the agent never
   rewrites creative text unasked.
5. **Apply, re-walk** every touched beat against its panel, run
   `uv run pytest -q tests/test_llm_story.py`, and update this file (closed
   holes, extended impressions, new ledger entries).
6. **Blind-check**: a subagent walks the touched arc with panels only (it must
   not open this canon, `docs/story.md`, or any source file) and reports what
   it understood. Verify every quote in its report against the files before
   acting — blind agents can misquote from false memory. Its report goes into
   the iteration notes; verified misses become new Open questions.
